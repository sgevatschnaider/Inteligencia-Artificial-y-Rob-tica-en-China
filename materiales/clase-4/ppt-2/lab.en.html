What this simulation models

For a stable M/M/1 queue:   W = 1 / (μ − λ)   with   λ < μ.

Total latency combines a hypothetical fixed transport/processing component with waiting and service time from the queueing model. If demand exceeds capacity, the simulator displays an overload and loss approximation; the steady-state M/M/1 formula is no longer used.

Important: The differences between 5G and 6G are adjustable teaching assumptions. They are not verified network measurements or promises of real latency. Satellite networks are also constrained by propagation, obstacles and coverage.

Critical interpretation

The illustrated 6G architecture includes integrated communications, AI and distributed processing. Lower latency does not depend only on the mobile generation; it also depends on service capacity, distance, interference and congestion.

Foundational reference: Kleinrock, Queueing Systems; ITU-R M.2160 (IMT-2030 framework). Simulator values are synthetic.

Simulator fundamentals and limits

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩   →   P(0) = |α|²,   P(1) = |β|²   y   |α|² + |β|² = 1.

For two qubits, the state space is {|00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩}. The model applies complex linear algebra for H, X, Z, S, Rz and CNOT gates. Prepare the Bell state from |00⟩ by applying H to the first qubit and then CNOT (control qubit 0).

The noise control uses a simplified uniform mixture in the measurement distribution: Pruido = (1−p)Pideal + p/2ⁿ. This does not simulate real quantum hardware or a complete physical channel. Correlations and the entanglement measure refer to the ideal state before noise.

Reference: Nielsen and Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information. Jiuzhang is a specialized photonic system; this gate model does not reproduce its boson-sampling experiments.

Suggested experiments

Experiment 1. Prepare |0⟩ and apply H. Measurement should approach 50% for 0 and 50% for 1.

Experiment 2. Prepare a Bell state: outcomes 00 and 11 should each appear close to 50%, with no 01 or 10 in the absence of noise.

Experiment 3. Increase noise and compare how the measurement distribution changes while keeping ideal unitary evolution unchanged.

Experiment 4. Apply Z to |+⟩. The distribution does not change immediately, but the relative phase does; the effect appears after another H.

1. 6G and Quantum Computing

Present two different frontiers. A 6G network aims to communicate, sense and coordinate services; a quantum processor transforms physical states to perform specific computational tasks. Both require research, standardization or validation and industrial ecosystems, but their adoption timelines differ. The photograph shows part of Jiuzhang 4.0, documented by Xinhua and published by CAS in May 2026. It represents neither a 6G network nor a universal fault-tolerant computer. Keep three questions visible throughout the module: what task was performed, what reference was used, and what conditions were required? This approach recognizes genuine advances without turning a specialized demonstration into a claim of general usefulness.

2. 5G and the IMT-2030 Framework

IMT-2030 is the ITU framework for mobile communications towards 2030 and beyond. Its scenarios include immersive communication, massive communication, ubiquitous connectivity, high reliability and low latency, AI and communication, and integrated sensing and communication. Distinguish use scenarios, target capabilities and available performance. A radio-interface latency figure does not automatically include transport, core networks, queues or application processing. Likewise, peak data rate is not a throughput guarantee for every user. Choose a use case such as industrial control or rural coverage and justify which capability matters and what cost it introduces. The slide summarizes four dimensions; these notes develop the six official categories so they are not reduced to a single race for speed.

3. Integrated Network Architecture

Follow a datum from a sensor to a decision. Radio access connects it to a station; transport and core manage resources and mobility; edge or cloud performs computation. Each segment adds delay, energy consumption and possible failure. Moving computation near the user can reduce part of the delay, but does not guarantee improvement if the node is congested. Satellites can extend coverage while adding propagation, visibility and capacity constraints. Chinese universities, manufacturers and operators participate with different interests. The architecture is conceptual rather than a description of one commercial network. Ask where students would place an inspection algorithm and what would change if the link to the data center failed.

4. Research, Standards and Deployment

The sequence distinguishes building a prototype, agreeing evaluation criteria, establishing an ecosystem and sustaining a service. The 2023 IMT-2030 framework organizes the vision; later work specifies requirements and evaluation methods. Participation by Chinese manufacturers and operators in research and standardization does not mean that a commercial 6G network is universally available. Deployment requires spectrum, devices, infrastructure, interoperability, operation and demand that can cover costs. Ask students to classify an announcement by its evidence: laboratory experiment, standards document, interoperability test or authorized service. Innovation can advance while these layers mature at different rates. The sequence does not establish guaranteed launch dates or future profitability.

5. A Qubit: Amplitude, Phase and Measurement

Write a qubit state as alpha times zero plus beta times one and explain probability normalization. Amplitude includes phase information that a single computational-basis measurement does not reveal directly. Consequently, two states with identical initial probabilities can behave differently after another gate. Apply H twice to zero to illustrate reversibility and interference. Entanglement is a property of a joint state; it does not enable instant messages or reveal every amplitude in a single measurement. The laboratory uses small ideal states and a noise mixture at measurement. This mixture does not reproduce decoherence, calibration or all errors in real hardware.

6. Quantum Platforms and Evidence

Compare platforms by task and protocol rather than by one headline number. Jiuzhang investigates photonic sampling; Zuchongzhi uses superconducting circuits and has published random-circuit-sampling experiments. The Zuchongzhi 3.0 paper describes a 105-physical-qubit processor; that does not mean 105 error-protected logical qubits. Photons, optical modes and gate-architecture qubits are not interchangeable quantities. Key distribution and metrology are other quantum applications. Ask what would count as evidence of usefulness for a specific industrial problem. A useful task, an updated classical comparison, error control and total cost would be needed in addition to the specialized demonstration.

7. From circuit to Bell state

Read the circuit from left to right. The initial state is zero zero. H on the first qubit prepares a superposition; CNOT uses that qubit as a control and changes the second when appropriate. The ideal final state is the sum of zero zero and one one divided by the square root of two. Repeated preparation and measurement yields approximately half of each outcome, rather than a guaranteed alternating sequence. Distinguishing correlation from entanglement requires knowledge of the protocol and may require measurements in other bases. In the laboratory, students can also undo gates, change the initial state and increase measurement noise. Ask for a prediction before comparing it with observed frequencies.

8. Algorithms: usefulness and requirements

Place each algorithm family next to its conditions for usefulness. Shor establishes a theoretical factoring result, but a practical threat to large-scale cryptography requires hardware and error correction that a sampling record does not establish. Grover provides a quadratic improvement in a query model; it does not remove the cost of building the oracle or loading data. Variational methods combine a circuit and classical optimization without guaranteeing industrial advantage. Quantum simulation aims to study quantum systems and also needs validation. Avoid promising acceleration for arbitrary AI tasks. The teaching criterion is to specify a task, a classical reference and a total resource budget before discussing advantage.

9. Waiting time rises near saturation

The chart is a queueing-theory calculation, not a measurement of 5G or 6G. For a stable M/M/1 queue, mean time in the system equals one divided by mu minus lambda. With capacity of one thousand requests per second, demands of 650, 900 and 990 yield approximately 2.86, 10 and 100 milliseconds. Proximity to saturation explains the nonlinear increase. Once lambda reaches or exceeds mu, that stationary regime does not exist; the laboratory switches to an overload approximation. Assumptions include Poisson arrivals, exponential service, one server and stability. Do not extrapolate these values to a real network without identifying topology, traffic distributions and additional delays.

    10. Predict, measure and compare

    Close by distinguishing physical laws, engineering simplifications and empirical evidence. In the network module, increasing demand demonstrates the nonlinear relation between latency and capacity. In the quantum simulator, preparing plus, applying Z and then H changes final probabilities even though Z does not immediately change the computational-basis distribution. For Bell, compare observed frequencies and theoretical predictions over many repetitions. Then add noise and explain which part comes from ideal evolution and which from the measurement model. The submission should state a prediction, record parameters and discuss a limitation. This structure connects experimentation with critical evaluation of technological announcements.

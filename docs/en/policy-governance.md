# AI Policy, Regulation, and Governance

**[Español](../es/politica-gobernanza.md) · [Back to the overview](../../README.en.md)**

## A multi-level strategy

Chinese AI policy combines national planning, local experimentation, industrial support, public procurement, platform regulation, and standards development. The State Council sets broad direction; ministries and regulators issue sectoral plans and rules; provincial and municipal governments compete for firms, talent, and infrastructure.

The 2017 New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan remains a central reference. Its target of roughly RMB 1 trillion for the core AI industry by 2030 describes expected industry value. It should not be cited as “USD 150 billion in government investment.”

The 2025 “AI Plus” initiative emphasises diffusion across science, manufacturing, consumption, public welfare, and governance. Its significance lies in the shift from capacity building towards the transformation of economic and organisational processes.

## Regulatory architecture

| Layer | Principal instruments | Regulatory question |
|---|---|---|
| Personal data | PIPL | Is there a lawful basis, data minimisation, and protection of individual rights? |
| Data security | Data Security Law and sectoral rules | How are data classified, stored, and transferred? |
| Information distribution | Algorithmic-recommendation rules | How do personalisation, filing, and content governance operate? |
| Synthetic content | Deep-synthesis and labelling rules | Can a person identify generated or altered content? |
| Generative AI | 2023 interim measures | What duties apply to services offered to the public? |
| Regulated sectors | Health, finance, automotive, and others | Which additional approvals and standards apply? |

The framework combines promotion and control. The generative-AI measures expressly pair “development and security” and limit their scope to services offered to the public in China. Internal research or production systems may be governed by other rules, but they are not automatically subject to every obligation imposed on public generative-AI services.

## Filing and assessment

Providers of services capable of shaping public opinion or social mobilisation may be subject to security assessment and algorithm filing. Filing is not blanket approval of every model output; it is an administrative mechanism whose significance depends on the service, provider, and applicable content obligations.

## International governance

China participates in the United Nations, standards bodies, and multilateral forums, promoting principles that include sovereignty, inclusive development, and a substantial role for states. At the same time, American and allied restrictions on advanced semiconductors, manufacturing equipment, and specified investments shape China's pursuit of technological self-reliance.

A balanced assessment separates:

1. the text of a policy;
2. the budgetary or regulatory instruments actually adopted;
3. corporate and local implementation;
4. observable outcomes; and
5. the geopolitical narratives advanced by each side.

## 15th Five-Year Plan, 2026–2030

The March 2026 official plan connects manufacturing upgrading, AI Plus, compute, algorithms, data and industrial capabilities. Chapters 12–14 address technology supply, applications and the development environment. The new-industry section includes integrated circuits, embodied intelligence and batteries.

Policy priorities are not measurements of completed adoption. Assess instruments, implementation and outcomes by industry, firm and location.

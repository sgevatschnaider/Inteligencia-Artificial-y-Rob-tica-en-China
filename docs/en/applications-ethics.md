# Applications, Ethics, and Society

**[Español](../es/aplicaciones-etica.md) · [Back to the overview](../../README.en.md)**

## From capability to impact

An AI application should be assessed through its function, data, affected population, routes for contesting decisions, and performance under real operating conditions. The fact that a system “uses AI” does not by itself establish productivity, fairness, or safety.

## Manufacturing

The most mature applications combine machine vision, process control, and robotics: inspection, predictive maintenance, production planning, energy management, and intralogistics. Benefits depend on data quality, integration with legacy equipment, and the ability of staff to intervene.

## Mobility

China brings together electric-vehicle manufacturers, automated-driving companies, mobility platforms, mapping providers, and local governments that authorise trials. Driver-assistance systems, conditional automation, and driverless services within defined operational domains must be treated as different categories.

Figures for kilometres driven or permits issued are not comparable when cities, weather, safety-operator requirements, and incident-reporting criteria differ.

## Healthcare and life sciences

Applications include medical-imaging support, triage, clinical documentation, drug discovery, hospital operations, and robotics. Regulatory approval, clinical validation, and workflow integration are stronger indicators than isolated laboratory performance.

Health data require attention to consent, purpose limitation, security, cross-population bias, and responsibility for error. A model assisting a clinician does not have the same risk profile as one determining access or treatment.

## Platforms, commerce, and work

Recommendation, advertising, customer-service, and content-generation systems operate at considerable scale. Their economic benefits coexist with risks involving manipulation, opacity, concentration, intellectual property, and pressure on platform workers and creators.

Labour analysis should measure transformed tasks, job quality, training, wages, and the distribution of gains—not merely jobs said to be “replaced” or “created.”

## Surveillance and recognition

China has deployed video surveillance and biometric recognition extensively. Analysis should identify the responsible authority, purpose, legal basis, retention, access, false-positive risk, and avenue for remedy. Broad language such as “ubiquitous surveillance” may convey a legitimate concern, but it does not substitute for evidence about a particular system.

## Social credit

There is no single national score determining every citizen's social standing. The framework includes regulatory information, compliance lists, judicial enforcement, corporate systems, and local pilots. It should also be distinguished from Zhima/Sesame Credit, Ant Group's commercial product.

Relevant concerns include proportionality of sanctions, data quality, notice, error correction, due process, and information reuse among agencies.

## Evaluation principles

1. **Specificity:** identify the system, actor, location, and period.
2. **Proportionality:** compare benefit with the severity and reversibility of harm.
3. **Contestability:** determine whether a person can understand, challenge, and correct a decision.
4. **Representativeness:** test data and performance across populations and contexts.
5. **Safety:** consider foreseeable failure, misuse, and operational dependence.
6. **Distribution:** examine who receives benefits, costs, and decision-making power.
